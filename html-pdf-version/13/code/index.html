The solutions for Patients Not Prisoners focused on advocating, educating, and improving systems to better support those who experience mental illness and the families who love them.

Solution #1: Advocate

  1. Engagement in Behavorial Health Consortiums/Council: Patients Not Prisoners seeks to engage so that incarceration prevention measures are fiscally and socially supported by the community. Patients Not Prisoners plans has a representative at the monthly council meetings to ensure their voice is heard.
  2. Advocating and Participating in the Mental Health Court: Patients Not Prisoners sees the need improve the current judicial system to better support people experiencing mental illness before and after incarceration. Patients Not Prisoners sits on the committee for the establishment of a “mental health” court to provide people experiencing mental illness access to diversion programs and options for wellness.
  3. Engage with Florida Mental Health Advocacy Coalition (FLMHAC) to support legislative priorites to improve the lives of those with Mental Illness and their families.

Solution #2: Educate

Patients Not Prisoners seeks to educate the community on the plight of mental illness and incarceration while educating parents and loved ones on how they can help those experiencing mental illness by:

  1. Hosting forum meetings, seminars and participating in public awareness venues.
  2. Partnering with the press to tell the story.
  3. Hosting and co-hosting Book Reviews, highlighting educational resources to educate our community.

Solution 3: Support

Patients Not Prisoners seeks to provide family support and access to mental health. We provide access to FREE therapy and restorative circles to families struggling to support their incarcerated loved ones. Our goal is to strengthen the cohesiveness of legacy systems/programs.

  1. Provide and Distribute Better Help vouchers for complementary 6 month therapy.
  2. Implement Legal Defense Fund to help those in need of legal services.
  3. Provide socialization and safe space events for the neurodivergent population.
  4. Answer phone calls and emails of hurting parents and loved ones.
  5. Engaging and igniting the community to initiate support through volunteerism and community activism to help those who are not able to help themselves.
