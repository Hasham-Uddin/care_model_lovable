Executive Summary

PAGE | 4

People with mental and substance use disorders are over-represented in the justice system. According to a 2024 SAMHSA report, It is estimated that 18% of the general population has a mental illness. However, an estimated 44% of those in jail and 37% of those in prison (PDF | 443 KB) have a mental illness. It is estimated that 11% of 18-25 year olds, and 6% of those over 25 years old have a substance use disorder. It is estimated that 63% of people in jail and 58% in prison have a substance use disorder. People with these disorders have challenges in getting appropriate treatment and often incarceration exacerbates their symptoms. This can lead to individuals staying incarcerated longer than those without behavioral health concerns.

Unfortunately, our country's largest psychiatric care providers are not hospitals but jails and prisons. These facilities are not well equipped to provide care for the mentally ill. The patchwork of the medical, social, and legal services meant to serve this population is profoundly fragmented and bureaucratic. It has limited formal methods of communicating and working with each other.

Patients Not Prisoners, (PNP's) program will create a comprehensive system of support for the mentally ill and their families within the framework of various community-based programs, coupled with advocacy within the judicial system. The program will advance civic advocacy, provide access to mental health care for families, including access to individual therapy and group/family restorative circles, and coordination with other community resources.

Our nonprofit has been created for and by people most impacted by the system of criminalization and mental illness. Representatives from our community worked together throughout 12 weeks to develop an evaluation plan to measure the program's outcomes. In order to ensure that their efforts are successful, Lisa developed this organization using the https://wemeasure.org Measure CARE Model as an evaluation tool. The Measure CARE Model focuses on creating sustainable change within communities by engaging community members and providing them with resources so they can advocate for themselves. Our work will advance several goals aimed at increasing the number of mentally ill people and their families participating in mental health programs and demonstrating improved mental health outcomes after receiving services.
