THEORY OF CHANGE

THEORY OF CHANGE

IF we provide meaningful advocacy, health, and supportive services for people experiencing mental illness who are at risk of, or currently incarcerated, THEN it will provide them the opportunity to reach their full potential of wellness, ultimately leading to a reduction in recidivism and the creation of safer, healthier communities.

LONG TERM GOAL

For mentally ill people to have the services, advocacy, and treatment that are needed so as to control the criminalization for maladaptive behaviors providing a safer community.

INTERMEDIATE GOALS

  • Strengthened partnerships and resource allocation to address mental health disparities.
  • Reduced disparities in legal outcomes for individuals with mental health conditions.
  • Broader public engagement in advocacy for systemic changes related to mental health and justice.
  • Improved support from loved ones for individuals experiencing mental health challenges.
  • Engage and ignite the community to initiate support through volunteerism.
  • Increased prioritization of mental health services in underserved communities.

SHORT TERM GOALS

  • Increased community awareness of mental health disparities and equity.
  • The Mental Health Court implementation phase is actively supported.
  • Individuals with mental health conditions access equitable legal representation.
  • Community members and stakeholders demonstrate increased understanding of the intersection of mental illness and incarceration.
  • Participation in forums and events grows, and feedback indicates an empathetic shift in public response.
  • Families access therapy and mental health resources.
  • Increased attendance and positive feedback at mental health awareness events.

ACTIVITIES

  • Engage in community councils to advocate for mental health equity and leverage data for impactful action.
  • Support the implementation phase of the Mental Health Court in collaboration with local organizations.
  • Provide financial and advocacy support for individuals with mental health conditions navigating the justice system through a Legal Defense Fund.
  • Host forum meetings, seminars, and public awareness events using storytelling to evoke empathy.
  • Partner with the press and utilize social media to amplify the stories of those affected by mental illness and incarceration.
  • Conduct collaborative book reviews to promote awareness and discussion of relevant topics.
  • Distribute free therapy vouchers to underserved families.
  • Host events to provide mental health resources and foster supportive environments.
  • Actively engage in legislative activites with FLMHAC.
  • Answer phone calls and emails of hurting parents and loved ones.

SOLUTION #1

Advocate for and Provide Better Resources and Support for Those Experiencing Mental Illness

SOLUTION #2

Educate the Community

SOLUTION #3

Support Families with Access to Mental Health Services

OUR TARGET POPULATION

  • Individuals experiencing mental illness, particularly those at risk of or currently involved in the justice system.
  • Families of individuals experiencing mental illness, especially in underserved communities.

ASSUMPTIONS

  • Adequate funding is available to support activities under each solution.
  • Communities and stakeholders are willing to collaborate and prioritize mental health equity.
  • Access to data and storytelling platforms effectively informs and engages the public
