Conclusion

Patients Not Prisoners used the data collected from the CARE Model to build out the foundation to provide neurodivergent people a chance to reach their full potential of wellness, thus reducing recidivism and providing safe, healthy communities. Through the CARE Model, Patients Not Prisoners was able to narrow the focus of the problem and their solutions.

Patients Not Prisoners has made significant strides in advancing our mission to improve mental health services and support within the justice system. Key achievements include securing official proclamations to designate May as Mental Health Awareness Month, amplifying awareness through heartfelt webinars, and hosting an International Book Review featuring a Pulitzer Prize-winning, bestselling author. Our advocacy efforts were bolstered by numerous editorials, television, and radio appearances, furthering public engagement with our cause. We proudly participated in the Sequential Intercept Mapping process to identify gaps and strengths in local behavioral health services and were honored to help lead the planning for our county’s first Mental Health Court. Additionally, we spearheaded a summit with the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) and the University of Florida Hospital, highlighting the critical role of supportive housing in mental health outcomes. Through these initiatives, we remain steadfast in our commitment to fostering systemic change, building healthier communities, and creating pathways to success for individuals impacted by mental illness and the criminal justice system.
